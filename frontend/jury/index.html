01THE FAILURE MODE

PROBLEM STATEMENT

A fast booking window makes two problems collide.

A high demand booking window rewards fast, repeated requests. Automated bots and real passengers can compete at the same rate. A blunt limit by IP address can block a whole campus or mobile carrier while distributed bots keep moving.

A / CONTENTION

Two groups. One seat.

Parallel requests must not produce duplicate tickets or leave half a family reservation behind.

B / FAIR ACCESS

Shared networks look like one user.

IP-only throttling can penalize passengers behind campus Wi-Fi, carrier NAT or public networks.

C / UNCERTAIN REPLIES

Committed, but not confirmed.

A dropped network response leaves the passenger unsure whether retrying will create another ticket.

03THE PROPOSED DEFENSE

WHAT FAIR ADMISSION NEEDS

Layer signals. Protect the seat at the write.

01

Verify before the rush

Passkeys or device-bound attestation can raise confidence in a session without putting an SMS round trip in the booking path.

02

Queue by journey

A per-train, date and class waiting room can cap the write-path load and give excess requests a predictable place to wait.

03

Admit without IP bias

Combine account, device, request-rate and abuse signals. An IP address is one signal, since many passengers can share one.

04

Allocate atomically

An atomic inventory operation reserves all requested seats or none. Idempotency keys and fencing versions make retries safe.

ARCHITECTURE PROPOSAL · FROM THE ATTACHED BLUEPRINT Virtual waiting rooms, passkeys, device attestation, Redis or actor-based inventory, payment sagas and async legacy sync are scale-up design ideas. The current college demo implements PostgreSQL seat locking, timed holds, group validation, mock payment and PNR recovery. It does not implement those production services.
